Educator and facilitator sheet

Build the Campus

A free browser game. Players are the construction manager for a data center campus on a made-up Georgia site and see how each choice moves the schedule, budget, crew safety and the neighbors. The game shows facts and trade-offs. It does not take a side on data centers.

  • For ages 12 and up
  • About 20 minutes per run
  • No sign-up, no personal data
  • Works offline after the first load

Who it is for

Ages 12 and up (grade 7 to adult); younger players with an adult. Best fit: grades 8–12 career-tech classes, introductory construction management, and adult civic sessions such as library panels on data centers.

Learning goals

After one run, players can:

  1. Put the nine phases of a construction project in order and explain why some work, like long-lead equipment, sets the pace (the critical path).
  2. Weigh a choice against schedule, budget, safety and neighbor effects, and say what each option gives up.
  3. Describe what a data center campus asks of a community (power, water, noise, traffic) and gives back (jobs, tax revenue), using facts rather than sides.

Running it

  • Library mode. On the first screen, tick Library mode · no voice. Narration is off, all text stays on screen, and the computer remembers it for the next player.
  • Class mode. Add ?mode=class to the game's web address. The game no longer starts over while players talk, and a reload keeps the game in progress. The end screen is the debrief: results against the plan, the three costliest choices and three questions, with a Print button. (Without class mode, an idle game starts over after about three minutes.)
  • Full screen. Press F11 (Windows, ChromeOS) or Ctrl+Cmd+F (Mac). On a projector, the four-corner map button (presenter view) enlarges the map and text.
  • Offline. Open the game once while online and a later reload works without a network. For a kiosk with no network, use the offline kiosk file (link in About).

Words to know

Critical path
the chain of tasks that sets the finish date; a delay on it delays the whole project.
Long-lead item
equipment, like a large transformer, that takes months or years to arrive, so it must be ordered early.
Silt fence and sediment basin
barriers and ponds that keep muddy rainwater on the site during earthwork.
MEP
mechanical, electrical and plumbing: the systems that power, cool and protect the building.
Switchgear
large electrical equipment that controls and protects the power feeding the building.
Commissioning
testing every system, in levels from factory checks to a planned power cut, before the owner takes over.
Megawatt (MW)
a unit of electric power; 100 MW is roughly the electricity of 84,000 homes.
Buffer and berm
the space (at least the setback) and earth mounds that screen a site from its neighbors.

Debrief questions

  1. Which choice cost your project the most? Would you make it again, and why?
  2. Why does the order of the work matter? What happens when long-lead equipment is ordered late?
  3. Different communities weigh these trade-offs differently. Which effect matters most where you live, and what facts would you want before deciding?

Privacy: the game collects no personal information. No sign-up, ads, analytics or cookies. Nothing players do is sent anywhere; the browser may remember settings and, in class mode, the game in progress until the tab closes. The host logs IP addresses for security.

The site, company and neighbors are made up; the rules and figures are real as of October 2026, with sources in the game’s About panel. Narration recorded in advance with ElevenLabs (Impact Program); the game contacts no service while you play.
