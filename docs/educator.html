Educator and facilitator sheet

Build the Campus

A free browser game. Players are the construction manager for a data center campus on a made-up Georgia site and see how each choice moves the schedule, budget, crew safety and the neighbors. The game shows facts and trade-offs. It does not take a side on data centers. Best fit: grades 8–12 career-tech, introductory construction management and adult civic sessions; younger players with an adult.

  • For ages 12 and up
  • About 20 minutes
  • No sign-up or personal data
  • Works offline once loaded
  • English only for now

Play: a2-learning-studio.github.io/data-center-build-playtest/
Class mode: a2-learning-studio.github.io/data-center-build-playtest/?mode=class

Learning goals

After one run, players can:

  1. Put the nine phases of a construction project in order and explain why some work, like long-lead equipment, sets the pace (the critical path).
  2. Weigh a choice against schedule, budget, safety and neighbor effects, and say what each option gives up.
  3. Describe what a data center campus asks of a community (power, water, noise, traffic) and gives back (jobs, tax revenue), using facts rather than sides.

Running it in class

  • Class mode. Use the class-mode address above: the game no longer starts over while players talk, and a reload keeps it until the tab closes. The end screen is the debrief (results, the costliest choices, three questions) with Print this summary and Save results file, a file with no name to hand in (Open results file shows it again).
  • Voice. Narration starts on. Use headphones, or press Voice: off (or V) on each device, which remembers it; &voice=off at the end of the address starts it off.
  • Full screen. F11 (Windows), the full-screen key in the top row (Chromebook) or Ctrl+Cmd+F (Mac). On a projector, presenter view (the four-corner map button, or &view=presenter) enlarges the map and text.
  • Issue cards bring weather, long-lead equipment, a labor shortage, a safety near miss (nobody is hurt), a late delivery or an inspection. Each answer trades time, money, safety or neighbor effects.

Library kiosk

With no mode, an idle game starts over after about three minutes, and the end screen clears after about a minute and a half.

  • Download the offline kiosk file (its address is in About) and open it in the browser’s kiosk or full-screen mode. It needs no network.
  • Add ?mode=library to its address for no narration. On a kiosk, the Library mode tick on the first screen lasts only that game.
  • Turn off sleep and the screen saver.
  • Print this summary opens the print dialog: connect a printer, or say the summary is for the screen.
  • Each morning, check the Play-test v1.5 badge; close other tabs.

Words to know

Critical path
the chain of tasks that sets the finish date; a delay on it delays the whole project.
Long-lead item
equipment, like a large transformer, that takes months or years to arrive, so it is ordered early.
Rezoning
a county’s decision, after a public hearing, on how land may be used. Counties can approve, change or deny it; the game approves.
Silt fence and sediment basin
barriers and ponds that keep muddy rainwater on the site.
MEP
mechanical, electrical and plumbing: the systems that power, cool and protect the building.
Switchgear
equipment that routes and protects the building’s power.
Commissioning
testing every system, in levels from factory checks to a planned power cut.
Megawatt (MW)
a unit of power; 100 MW used all year is about 84,000 U.S. homes’ electricity. Its emissions depend on how it is generated.
Buffer and berm
the space (at least the setback) and earth mounds between a site and its neighbors.

Debrief questions

  1. Which choice cost your project the most? Would you make it again, and why?
  2. Why does the order of the work matter? What happens when long-lead equipment is ordered late?
  3. Different communities weigh these trade-offs differently. Which effect matters most where you live, and what facts would you want before deciding?

Privacy: no personal information, sign-up, ads, analytics or cookies; nothing is sent anywhere. The browser may keep the Library mode, presenter view and shortcut settings; in class and home modes, also the voice setting, and the game in progress until the tab closes. The host logs IP addresses for security.

The site, company and neighbors are made up; the rules and Neighbors facts are real as of October 2026 (sources in About), and costs, delays and points are simplified game values. Narration recorded in advance with ElevenLabs (Impact Program); the game contacts no service while you play.
